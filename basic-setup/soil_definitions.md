# Soil and Agriculture Definitions

- **Soil**: The top layer of the earth composed of minerals, organic matter, water, air, and living organisms; it supports plant growth by supplying nutrients and anchoring roots.
- **Agriculture**: The science and practice of cultivating soil, growing crops, and raising livestock to produce food, fiber, and other products.
- **Crop**: A plant or plant product grown and harvested for consumption, trade, or processing (e.g., wheat, rice, maize, cotton).
- **Irrigation**: The deliberate application of water to soil through methods such as drip lines, sprinklers, or flood channels to maintain adequate moisture for plant growth.
- **Soil Fertility**: The soil’s ability to supply essential nutrients in adequate amounts and proper balance for healthy plant growth.
- **Soil Texture**: The relative proportion of sand, silt, and clay particles in soil, influencing water retention, drainage, and aeration.
- **Soil Structure**: The arrangement of soil particles into aggregates, affecting porosity, water movement, root penetration, and aeration.
- **Organic Matter**: Decomposed plant and animal residues in soil that improve structure, water-holding capacity, and nutrient availability.
- **Compost**: Decomposed organic materials used to enrich soil with nutrients and beneficial microorganisms.
- **Mulch**: A protective layer of organic or inorganic material placed on the soil surface to conserve moisture, suppress weeds, and moderate temperature.
- **Erosion**: The removal and transport of soil by water, wind, or gravity, often accelerated by deforestation, overgrazing, or poor land management.
- **Conservation Tillage**: Farming practices that minimize soil disturbance (e.g., no-till, strip-till) to reduce erosion and maintain soil structure and organic matter.
- **Cover Crop**: A crop grown primarily to protect and improve soil (e.g., legumes for nitrogen fixation), not for direct sale or consumption.
- **Soil pH**: A measure of soil acidity or alkalinity; influences nutrient availability and microbial activity. Most crops prefer a pH between 6.0 and 7.5.
- **Nutrient Management**: Planning and applying fertilizers, manures, and amendments to meet crop needs while minimizing environmental impacts.
- **Drainage**: The removal of excess water from soil to prevent waterlogging and root suffocation, using surface ditches or subsurface tiles.
- **Salinity**: The concentration of soluble salts in soil or water; high salinity can inhibit plant growth and requires management through leaching or salt-tolerant crops.
- **Soil Compaction**: The compression of soil particles, reducing pore space and limiting root growth and water infiltration; often caused by heavy machinery on wet soils.
- **Rhizosphere**: The narrow region of soil directly influenced by root secretions and associated microorganisms, critical for nutrient exchange.
- **Agroforestry**: Integrating trees or shrubs with crops and/or livestock to enhance biodiversity, soil health, and microclimate regulation.
